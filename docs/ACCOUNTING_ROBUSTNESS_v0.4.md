# Cost-Accounting Robustness v0.4

## Primary and secondary estimands

The primary estimand charges the full expected cost when a feasible attempt
starts. The secondary estimand charges activities only when their stage is
reached.

Both architectures keep cost outside adoption utility and all process
probabilities.

## Why the conventions differ

Under staged accounting, a rejected referral request consumes only the
identify and request shares. A failed direct contact can also avoid later
follow-up cost. The remaining budget may finance additional attempts.

The default 0.25 shares are diagnostic bookkeeping values, not empirical
activity shares.

## Results

The original 50-replication coarse gate estimated baseline thresholds of 1.00
and 1.25. The refined 400-replication analysis estimates 1.20 and 1.25.
Therefore, most of the original baseline gap reflected coarse resolution and
Monte Carlo variation.

| Condition | Upfront \(r^*\) | Staged \(r^*\) | Difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Baseline | 1.20 | 1.25 | 0.05 |
| Budget abundant | 1.15 | 1.40 | 0.25 |
| Budget scarce | 1.10 | 1.20 | 0.10 |
| Referrers abundant | 1.05 | 1.15 | 0.10 |
| Referrers scarce | 1.25 | 1.25 | 0.00 |
| Sociality high | 1.40 | 1.50 | 0.10 |
| Acceptance low | No region | No region | — |

## Decision

Upfront expected cost remains the primary architecture. Staged accounting is
reported separately and must not be treated as a calibrated alternative.

The abundant-budget difference reaches the predeclared material threshold of
0.25. Consequently:

- no definitive economic conclusion is based on staged accounting;
- no Consumat extension is authorized;
- future evidence must estimate the activity shares and the economic value of
  entrepreneurial time; and
- the empirical template must compare the resulting interval for \(r\) with
  both accounting estimands.
